Field Notes

What Paul gravitates to.

Paul doesn't watch horror for the scare. He watches for the moment right before he can name what's wrong.

Across this catalog, cosmic dread is the single largest thread — forces and physics that don't care about the people caught inside them: a comet, a signal, a hole in the ground, a ship that folds space and comes back wrong. The horror isn't a monster wearing a mask. It's a rule of the universe he didn't know was breakable.

Right behind it, in a near-tie, are two threads that keep pace with each other: bodies and identities that stop being singular — doubles, possessions, minds split down the middle — and confinement over open space: a cabin, a hotel, a cube, a dinner party he can't politely leave. Folded reality and time loops round out a strong fourth, and a growing pocket of first-contact signals shows he's just as drawn to something communicating from beyond as he is to something simply lurking there. Jump scares are rare across all of it. Dread accrues.

The scores tell an interesting story as the catalog has grown: the earliest additions leaned toward moody, divisive cult picks where Metacritic's measured middle diverged sharply from Rotten Tomatoes' binary fresh-or-rotten count. As Paul has added more stone-cold classics — Alien, 2001, Close Encounters, Solaris — the average score has climbed and that critic/audience gap has narrowed. He's not just drawn to divisive art-house dread; he's just as comfortable with the canon everyone already agrees is great.

Field Notes, Continued

What doesn't make the cut.

A taste is defined as much by what it declines as by what it keeps.

Torture porn

Suffering staged as spectacle rather than atmosphere.

Jump-scare franchises

A loud noise isn't a scare, it's a startle.

Over-explained mythology

The instant a film hands over the answer key, the dread evaporates.

Body-count slashers

A masked killer working through a cast list isn't a mystery, it's a schedule.

Horror played for laughs

If the tone is mostly punchlines, the dread never gets room to build.

CGI creature spectacle

Big-budget monsters with scale but no mood.
